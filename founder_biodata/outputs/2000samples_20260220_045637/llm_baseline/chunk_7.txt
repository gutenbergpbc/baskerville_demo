As an analyst reviewing this batch of 80 founder profiles (of which 15 were provided in detail), I have identified several high-signal patterns that distinguish the $20M+ exits from the $0–$1M outcomes.

### 1. Features of High-Exit Founders ($20M+)
High-exit founders in this data set (Founders 483, 493, 496, 513, 519, 525, 530, 552, 559) share a specific "Systems Architect" profile.

*   **The "Chief of Staff" or "VP" at Scale (Frequency: ~80% of high exits):** Nearly every high-exit founder spent 3–5 years in a high-leverage, "connective tissue" role at a Decacorn (Meta, Netflix, Stripe, SpaceX, Amazon). They didn't just code; they saw how massive systems fail and how to navigate internal politics.
*   **The "Translation Layer" Skillset:** High-exit founders are almost always described as being able to "bridge the gap" between deep technical architecture (ML/NLP/Distributed Systems) and Enterprise Sales/C-Suite strategy.
*   **Unsexy "Plumbing" Focus:** High exits correlate with solving "friction" in boring industries (Logistics, HR Infrastructure, Legal Discovery, Healthcare Interoperability) rather than consumer-facing "apps."
*   **Advanced Degrees as "Logic Frameworks":** High-exit founders often hold MDs or PhDs (Chemistry, Neuroscience, Physics) but *do not practice*. They use the degree as a mental model for "biological systems" or "complex logic" which they then apply to software.
*   **Specific Hobbies (Pattern Recognition):** High-exit profiles frequently mention **Chess, Go, or Bridge**. These games require probabilistic thinking under imperfect information, mirroring high-stakes scaling.

### 2. Features of Low/Zero-Exit Founders
Low-exit founders (Founders 481, 482, 485, 490, 492, 494, 503, 507, 510, 515, 523, 527, 541) often fall into the "Pure Craftsman" or "Academic Purist" traps.

*   **The "Hands-on" CEO Trap:** Low-exit founders are often praised for "still submitting pull requests" or "leading from the IDE" even at Series A/B stages. While admirable, this suggests a failure to scale the organization beyond their own technical output.
*   **Over-Indexing on "Scrappiness":** Founders who take pride in "doing more with less" or "skepticism of over-capitalization" (e.g., Founder 482, 492) often fail to capture the market aggressively enough to reach a $20M+ liquidity event.
*   **The "Visionary" without the "Operator":** Profiles that focus heavily on "painting a picture of the future" or "philosophical provocations" without a background in high-scale operations (e.g., Founder 527, 504) tend to result in $0 exits.
*   **Tactile/Solitary Hobbies:** There is a surprising correlation between **Woodworking, Pottery, and Sculpture** and $0 exits. While these show "attention to detail," they are solitary, slow-feedback-loop activities that may reflect a founder's preference for perfectionism over market velocity.

### 3. Surprising & Non-Obvious Patterns
*   **The "History Buff" Signal:** Founders who are described as "History Buffs" or who "draw parallels to the 19th-century industrial revolution" appear frequently in both categories, but in **high-exit** profiles, they use history to predict **market cycles**, whereas in **low-exit** profiles, they use it to justify **product philosophy**.
*   **The "Music" Divergence:**
    *   **High Exit:** Classical Violin/Piano (Requires rigid adherence to complex, pre-existing scores/systems).
    *   **Low/Zero Exit:** DJing, Saxophone, or Music Production (Focuses on individual expression, "vibe," and improvisation).
*   **The "Public Speaking" Paradox:** High-exit founders are often described as "quiet," "listening until the air runs out," or "low-frequency." The "high-energy," "charismatic" speakers in this batch (Founders 507, 534, 541) almost universally resulted in $0 exits.
