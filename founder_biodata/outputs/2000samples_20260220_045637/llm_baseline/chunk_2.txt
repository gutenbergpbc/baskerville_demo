Based on an analysis of the 80 founder profiles provided, here is the breakdown of patterns correlating with high exit amounts ($20M+) versus small or zero exits.

### 1. High-Exit Predictors ($20M+)
These features appear with high frequency in the bios of founders with significant exits (e.g., Founders 82, 87, 94, 99, 113, 133).

*   **The "Apprenticeship in Scale" (Big Tech Mid-Management):** High-exit founders almost universally spent 3–5 years at a "Scale-up Giant" (Salesforce, Stripe, Netflix, Apple, or Uber) in roles like **VP of Engineering, VP of Product, or Strategy Consultant.**
    *   *Observation:* They didn't just work there; they "obsessed over friction points" or "translated logistical nightmares into technical requirements."
*   **The "Unsexy" Infrastructure Focus:** High exits are heavily correlated with founders solving "plumbing" problems—supply chain optimization, data interoperability, or backend financial settlement layers—rather than consumer-facing apps.
*   **Elite Technical/Analytical Education + Career Pivot:** A recurring pattern is a rigorous degree (Mathematics at Oxford, Physics at Princeton, Mechanical Engineering at MIT) followed by a pivot into a different high-stakes field (e.g., MD to Fintech, or Physics to HRTech).
*   **Specific Hobbies (The "Tactile/Systemic" Balance):** High-exit founders often engage in hobbies that require precise, slow-motion feedback loops:
    *   **Restoring Vintage Hardware/Computing** (Commodore 64s, early Macs).
    *   **Technical Music** (Saxophone, Piano, Music Production).
    *   **Tactile Crafts** (Pottery, Sourdough Baking, 3D Printing).
*   **Communication Style:** Described as "listening more than talking," "consensus builders," or "disarming." They lead with "how" rather than "why."

### 2. Low or Zero-Exit Predictors ($0 - $1M)
These features appear frequently in the bios of founders with $0 exits or very small outcomes (e.g., Founders 81, 88, 92, 107, 124, 130).

*   **The "Chief of Staff" Trap:** While a prestigious title, founders whose *primary* leadership experience was a "Chief of Staff" role (at SpaceX, Tesla, or YC startups) without a subsequent deep-tier VP/Director role in a specific function often resulted in $0 exits. They are "generalists" who struggle when the "scrappy" phase ends.
*   **The "Move Fast and Break Things" Archetype:** Bios that emphasize "high-velocity," "kinetic energy," "moving at a sprint," or "abrasive directness" (e.g., Founders 92, 96, 107) correlate strongly with failure. These founders often "break things that are legally or structurally essential."
*   **Narrative-Driven vs. Data-Driven:** Founders described as "storytellers" or those who "lead with a story rather than a slide deck" (Founder 81) frequently failed. High-exit founders were conversely described as "allergic to abstraction."
*   **Academic Over-Specialization:** Founders who stayed in academia for a PhD and then immediately founded a company, or those who treat the company as a "science project" (Founder 130), show lower exit rates than those who spent time in "industrial schooling" (corporate roles).
*   **Aggressive/Combative Personalities:** Founders described as "unswayed by egos," "blunt," or "intimidating" (Founders 115, 120) often had $0 or very low exits, suggesting difficulty in long-term team retention or partnership building.

### 3. Surprising & Non-Obvious Signals
*   **The "Crossword/Puzzle" Correlation:** A surprising number of founders with small but non-zero exits ($40k–$900k) are obsessed with **cryptic crosswords** or **chess**. While this shows intelligence, it may correlate with a "puzzle-solving" mindset that prioritizes the elegance of the solution over the commercial scale of the business.
*   **Geography as a Counter-Indicator:** Founders who moved to "lifestyle" or "emerging" hubs (Nashville, Sydney, Portland) to "escape the noise" of the Valley/London had a higher failure rate in this batch than those who stayed in the "friction" of major hubs (Boston, SF, London, Tel Aviv).
*   **The "Boxing/Martial Arts" Signal:** Founders who mention boxing or CrossFit (Founders 92, 100, 114, 119) are high-energy but have highly volatile outcomes—either massive exits ($169M) or $0. It is a "high-beta" personality trait.
