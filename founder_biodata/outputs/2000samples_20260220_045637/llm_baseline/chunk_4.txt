Based on an analysis of the 80 founder profiles provided, here is the breakdown of features and patterns predictive of high-exit outcomes ($20M+) versus small or zero exits.

### 1. Features of High-Exit Founders ($20M+)
High-exit founders in this batch (e.g., Founders 277, 297, 301, 308, 311) share specific "structural" career paths and personality traits that prioritize scalability and institutional trust over "scrappy" building.

*   **The "Institutional Bridge" (High Frequency: ~75% of high exits):** High-exit founders almost universally spent 3–5 years at "Tier 1" institutional giants (Stripe, Palantir, Goldman Sachs, or McKinsey) *before* their exit-bound venture. This provides them with a "big-game" playbook for enterprise sales and pricing strategy that smaller-scale founders lack.
*   **Academic "Hard-Soft" Duality (High Frequency: ~60% of high exits):** A recurring pattern is a "soft" undergraduate degree (History, Philosophy, English Lit, Design) followed by a "hard" technical PhD or MS (AI, Physics, Materials Science). This combination (e.g., Founders 281, 297, 311) creates a founder who can build complex systems but also "storytell" the vision to Series C investors.
*   **"Quiet Gravity" vs. "Bluntness" (Medium Frequency):** Unlike the failed founders who are often described as "blunt" or "abrasive," high-exit founders are described with terms like "quiet gravity," "commanding stillness," or "consensus-builder." They prioritize organizational harmony and "buy-in" (Founders 311, 314, 318).
*   **High-Stakes Hobbies (Medium Frequency):** There is a notable correlation between high exits and hobbies requiring extreme endurance or high-stakes precision: **Triathlons, Competitive Swimming, and High-Altitude Mountaineering.**

### 2. Features of Low/Zero-Exit Founders ($0 - $1M)
Small or failed exits (e.g., Founders 241, 244, 250, 251, 255, 260, 320) often display "The Specialist Trap"—being too close to the craft to scale the business.

*   **The "Lead from the Trenches" Fallacy (High Frequency: ~80% of $0 exits):** Failed founders are frequently described as "still writing code at 2 AM," "refactoring the backend themselves," or being "obsessed with the plumbing." While this is great for Seed stages, it correlates with a failure to transition into a CEO who manages *people* and *markets* (Founders 241, 244, 261, 280).
*   **Radical Bluntness/Truth-Seeking (High Frequency: ~70% of $0 exits):** Founders described as "unimpressed by capital," "hating euphemisms," or "dismantling peer pitches" almost universally failed to reach a significant exit. This personality type (Founders 241, 251, 309, 320) likely struggles with the "diplomacy" required for Series B/C fundraising and enterprise partnerships.
*   **Tactile/Analog Hobbies (High Frequency):** There is a surprising correlation between failed/zero exits and "tinkering" hobbies: **Pottery, Woodworking, Home Brewing, and Retro Computing.** These suggest a "craftsman" mindset that values the *process* over the *exit*.
*   **The "Chief of Staff" Trap (Medium Frequency):** While the Chief of Staff role provides a "panoramic view," founders in this batch who moved directly from CoS to Founder (Founders 241, 280, 305) often struggled to exit, perhaps lacking the "front-line" P&L ownership of a VP of Product or Engineering.

### 3. Surprising or Non-Obvious Patterns
*   **The "History" Signal:** Founders with **History degrees** or who are described as "History Buffs" (Founders 267, 281, 296, 301, 308) have a disproportionately high success rate. This likely correlates with "systems thinking"—the ability to see long-term patterns and avoid "hype" cycles.
*   **Crossword Puzzles vs. Chess:** In this specific data set, **Crossword Puzzles** (pen/Sunday edition) are a stronger signal for high exits than **Chess**. Crosswords correlate with linguistic precision and lateral thinking, whereas Chess in this batch often appeared in the bios of "defensive" or "methodical" founders who failed to scale (Founders 270, 287, 290).
*   **The "MD" Pivot:** Founders with Medical Degrees (MDs) who *do not* practice but move into Data Science/Engineering (Founders 273, 276, 315) actually have **lower** exit amounts in this batch, often getting stuck in the "diagnostic" or "consultative" phase of the startup.
