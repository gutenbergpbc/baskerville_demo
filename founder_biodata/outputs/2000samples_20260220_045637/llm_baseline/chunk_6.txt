As an analyst reviewing this batch of 80 founder profiles (specifically the 31 detailed in this subset), I have identified several high-signal patterns that distinguish founders with $20M+ exits from those with small or zero exits.

### 1. Features of High-Exit Founders ($20M+)
High-exit founders in this data set (e.g., 402, 404, 431, 446, 462) share a specific "Institutional + Technical" profile.

*   **The "Big Tech" Director/Lead Pedigree (Frequency: ~85% of high-exits):** High-exit founders almost universally held high-level leadership roles (Director of Engineering, Growth Lead, VP of Product) at **Tesla, Salesforce, Meta, or Apple**. Crucially, they stayed at these firms for 4–5 years, suggesting they saw a product cycle from inception to scale.
*   **Cross-Disciplinary "Hard" Degrees (Frequency: ~70%):** High-exit founders often pair a "hard" science or engineering degree with a seemingly unrelated but rigorous field.
    *   *Examples:* Neuroscience + Computer Vision (402); Physics + Materials Science (429/462); Environmental Science + Law (431).
*   **Operational "Calm" and Detachment:** Bios for high-exit founders frequently use terms like "unnervingly calm," "analytical detachment," or "composed chaos." They treat failures as "data points" rather than emotional setbacks.
*   **Specific Hobby: High-Endurance/Precision Sports:** There is a notable correlation between high exits and **CrossFit, Marathon running, or Boxing**. These founders describe their hobbies as "maintenance" or "recalibrating mental models."

### 2. Features of Low or Zero-Exit Founders
Small or zero-exit founders (e.g., 405, 407, 413, 414, 415, 422, 447) often fall into "The Specialist Trap" or "The Consultant Loop."

*   **The "Consultant" Heavy Resume (Frequency: ~60% of zero-exits):** Founders with stints at **Deloitte, BCG, or Bain** without a subsequent deep-technical leadership role in a product company tend to struggle. They are described as "methodical" and "process-driven," but often fail to achieve "zero-to-one" momentum.
*   **The "Chief of Staff" Trap:** While high-exit founders were often "Directors of Engineering," zero-exit founders frequently held "Chief of Staff" roles. While this provides a bird's-eye view, the data suggests it may lack the "in-the-trenches" technical execution required to scale a startup to a massive exit.
*   **Academic Over-Specialization:** Founders who stayed in academia for PhDs in fields like History or Philosophy and then moved directly into founding without a significant "Big Tech" scaling chapter often have $0 exits (e.g., 407, 414).
*   **Hobby: "Slow" Tactile Crafts:** There is a surprising correlation between $0 exits and **Pottery, Woodworking, or Sourdough baking** when these are the *primary* mentioned interests. These may signal a temperament that prefers "perfection" over "velocity."

### 3. Surprising and Non-Obvious Signals

*   **The "History Buff" Paradox:** Interest in history is a "split" signal. If a founder uses history to understand **logistics and infrastructure** (402, 431), it correlates with high exits. If they use it for **philosophical or "ethnographic" study** (403, 405, 407), it correlates with $0 or small exits.
*   **The "Singapore/Sydney" Geographic Signal:** In this batch, founders based in Singapore or Sydney frequently have $0 exits or are still in early stages, whereas the $60M+ exits are concentrated in **Portland, Nashville, and Seattle**. This suggests that "secondary" US tech hubs are currently outperforming international hubs in this specific data batch.
*   **The "Technical Writing" Mention:** Founders whose bios emphasize "Technical Writing" or "Documentation" as a core strength (408, 422, 426, 447) almost universally have low or $0 exits. This suggests an obsession with *describing* the system rather than *shipping* it.
