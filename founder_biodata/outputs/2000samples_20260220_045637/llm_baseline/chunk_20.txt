Based on an analysis of the 80 founder profiles provided, here is the data-driven breakdown of features predictive of high-exit outcomes ($20M+) versus small or zero exits.

### 1. Features Predictive of High-Exit Outcomes ($20M+)
High-exit founders in this batch (e.g., Founders 1522, 1535, 1541, 1552, 1561, 1566, 1591, 1600) share several distinct clusters of characteristics:

*   **The "Practitioner-Founder" Archetype (Frequency: ~85% of high exits):** High-exit founders almost universally spent 10–20 years inside "Big Tech" or high-stakes environments (Microsoft, Amazon, Goldman Sachs, SpaceX, Uber) before founding. They are rarely first-time "dreamers"; they are seasoned operators who identified a specific "plumbing" failure while working at scale.
*   **Multidisciplinary "Hard-Soft" Pivots (Frequency: ~70% of high exits):** A recurring pattern is a "hard" technical degree (Physics, Math, Statistics, Engineering) paired with a "soft" professional pivot (Strategy Consulting at Bain/McKinsey or a JD/MBA). This allows them to speak "Engineer" and "C-Suite" simultaneously, which is critical for the enterprise sales cycles seen in the $80M+ exits.
*   **Obsession with "Unsexy" Infrastructure (Frequency: ~90% of high exits):** High exits are concentrated in **Cybersecurity, InsurTech, and Logistics/Supply Chain**. These founders focus on "boring" problems like regulatory compliance, data integrity, and backend latency rather than consumer-facing "visionary" apps.
*   **Specific Hobby Correlation: Competitive/Analytical Play:** High-exit founders frequently mention **Competitive Chess, Bridge, Go, or Martial Arts**. These hobbies correlate with a high tolerance for "positional" pressure and the ability to think several moves ahead in a regulated environment.
*   **"Quiet Intensity" vs. "Founder Ego":** High-exit bios repeatedly use descriptors like "measured," "quietly intense," "low-ego," and "comfortable with silence." They lead by "auditing" rather than "manifesting."

### 2. Features Predictive of Low or Zero-Exit Outcomes
Small or failed exits (e.g., Founders 1525, 1526, 1527, 1531, 1533, 1544, 1551, 1562, 1567, 1582) often exhibit these "false signals":

*   **The "Academic/Philosopher" Trap:** While a humanities background can be a strength, founders who remain too "theoretical" (e.g., Founders 1527, 1531, 1582) often fail. If the bio emphasizes "deconstructing metaphysical properties" or "Kantian ethics" over "raw SQL queries" or "unit economics," the exit amount is frequently $0.
*   **The "Over-Educated" Generalist:** Founders with three or more disparate degrees (e.g., Philosophy + Law + Bio-Engineering) without a long tenure in a single industry often struggle to reach the $20M mark. They appear to be "professional students" of systems rather than builders of them.
*   **"Visionary" Keynote Speakers:** Founders described as "visionary," "charismatic," or "favorites on the speaker circuit" (e.g., Founders 1534, 1544) often have lower exits. This suggests they may prioritize the *narrative* of the startup over the *grind* of the infrastructure.
*   **High-Burn/High-Capital Sectors (SpaceTech/CleanTech):** In this specific batch, founders in capital-intensive fields like SpaceTech (Founders 1527, 1565) or CleanTech (Founders 1532, 1568) show a high failure rate ($0 exits), likely due to the "Series B crunch" where technical milestones fail to meet capital requirements.

### 3. Surprising or Non-Obvious Patterns
*   **The "History Buff" Signal:** Surprisingly, a personal interest in **History/Military Logistics** is a strong positive signal for high exits (Founders 1522, 1591, 1600). It suggests a founder who understands that systems are prone to "institutional inertia" and builds for long-term resilience.
*   **The "Tactile Hobby" Balance:** High-exit founders often have "analog" hobbies that require precision: **Woodworking, Pottery, or Sourdough Baking**. This suggests a "prototyping" mindset—the ability to handle "messy" physical variables and iterate until a result is repeatable.
*   **Geography as a Filter:** Founders who deliberately chose "non-Valley" hubs like **Chicago, Sydney, or Bangalore** to avoid the "echo chamber" (Founders 1522, 1535, 1591, 1600) outperformed those in the heart of the SF/Palo Alto scene in this batch.
