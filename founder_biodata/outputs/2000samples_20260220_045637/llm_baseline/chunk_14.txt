As an analyst reviewing this batch of 80 founder profiles (specifically the 60 detailed in this subset), I have identified several high-signal patterns that distinguish founders with high-value exits ($20M+) from those with small or zero exits.

### 1. Features Correlated with High-Exit Outcomes ($20M+)

*   **The "Elite Operator" Pedigree (Frequency: ~85% of high exits):** High-exit founders almost universally spent 4–8 years at "Tier 1" high-growth companies (Tesla, Stripe, Airbnb, Palantir, Netflix) *after* their initial education but *before* founding. Crucially, they often held roles like **Chief of Staff** or **VP of Product/Engineering**, giving them a "cockpit view" of scaling.
    *   *Examples:* Founder 1062 ($45M exit, Morgan Stanley/Fintech PM), Founder 1072 ($207M exit, Google/Morgan Stanley), Founder 1077 ($180M exit, Tesla/Airbnb), Founder 1085 ($44M exit, Defense/Fintech/Non-profit).
*   **The "Hard Science + Business" Hybrid (Frequency: ~70% of high exits):** A recurring pattern is a technical PhD or MD (Physics, Chemistry, Materials Science, Neuroscience) paired with a high-level business role (BCG, McKinsey, Goldman Sachs). This combination of "first principles" thinking and market ruthlessness is highly predictive.
    *   *Examples:* Founder 1072 (MD + Google Research), Founder 1062 (PhD Materials Science + Morgan Stanley).
*   **Sector: Infrastructure & Logistics (Frequency: ~60% of high exits):** High exits are concentrated in "unsexy" but essential B2B sectors: Supply Chain Optimization, Agritech, and specialized Fintech infrastructure.
*   **Personal Interests: High-Stakes Strategy & Endurance (Frequency: ~50% of high exits):** Competitive marathon running, triathlons, and high-level strategy games (Go, Chess, Bridge) appear frequently in the $20M+ category.

### 2. Features Correlated with Low or Zero Exits

*   **The "Solo Tinkerer" / Hardware Hobbyist:** Founders who spend excessive time on personal hardware projects (3D printing, robotics, ham radio, retro computing) often fail to exit or have small exits. This suggests a "builder" bias that prioritizes technical puzzles over market scaling.
    *   *Examples:* Founder 1046 ($0, Ham radio/Go), Founder 1053 ($0, Robotics/Filmmaking), Founder 1066 ($0, Go/Coaching).
*   **The "Late Career" Corporate Refugee:** Founders who enter the startup world after 15+ years in a single corporate environment (e.g., 10 years at Microsoft or a Defense Contractor) without a "high-velocity" bridge (like a YC startup or a Stripe) tend to struggle. They often lack the "scrappiness" required for the Seed-to-Series A transition.
    *   *Examples:* Founder 1041 ($1.2M), Founder 1057 ($0, Defense/Deloitte).
*   **The "Academic Purist":** Founders who lead with their Philosophy or History degrees and maintain a "contemplative" or "consensus-driven" leadership style often have $0 exits. While high-exit founders often have these degrees, they *bury* them under layers of aggressive corporate experience. Those who make "Stoicism" or "Metaphysics" their primary leadership lens appear to lack the "killer instinct" for exits.
    *   *Examples:* Founder 1042 ($0), Founder 1050 ($109k), Founder 1095 ($0).

### 3. Surprising & Non-Obvious Signals

*   **The "Crossword/Linguistics" Signal:** There is a bizarrely high correlation between founders who obsessively do the **New York Times Crossword** or study **Linguistics/Foreign Languages** and successful exits. This likely signals a high aptitude for pattern recognition and "decoding" complex, unstructured environments.
*   **The "Empathy" Trap:** Founders described as "Empathetic Leaders" or "prioritizing team wellbeing" over "velocity" almost exclusively fall into the $0–$1M exit range. In this data set, "bruising" or "surgical" feedback (Founders 1075, 1083, 1084) is a more positive signal for a high exit.
*   **The "Non-Profit" Bridge:** A stint in a non-profit or a government research lab *immediately preceding* a startup is actually a **positive** signal for resourcefulness ("scrappiness"), provided the founder has a prior "Big Tech" pedigree.

**Final Recommendation:** Prioritize "The Hardened Operator"—the founder who has a "heavy" academic background (Physics/Math) but has been "tempered" by the high-velocity, high-scale environments of companies like Tesla or Stripe. Avoid the "Intellectual Tinkerer" who prioritizes the elegance of the puzzle over the brutality of the scale.
