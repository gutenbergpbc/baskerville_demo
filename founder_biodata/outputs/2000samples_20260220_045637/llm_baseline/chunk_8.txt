As an analyst reviewing this batch of 80 founder profiles (specifically the subset of 80 provided in this sample), I have identified several high-signal patterns that differentiate founders with high exits ($20M+) from those with small or zero exits.

### 1. High-Exit Founder Features ($20M+)
Founders in the high-exit bracket (e.g., Founder 564, 566, 619, 640) share specific professional and personal markers:

*   **The "Big Tech" Technical Lead/Director Pivot:** High-exit founders almost universally spent 3–5 years in high-level engineering or product roles at **Tesla, Google, Meta, or Apple**. Crucially, they often held titles like "Technical Lead," "Director of Engineering," or "Senior Software Engineer" (e.g., Founder 564 at McKinsey/ASU, Founder 566 at JPMorgan/BCG, Founder 619 at Tesla/Google).
*   **The "Hard Science" Academic Foundation:** High exits correlate strongly with undergraduate degrees in **Physics, Chemistry, or Electrical Engineering** (e.g., Founder 619 - Physics, Founder 640 - Psychology/Biotech). These founders frame business problems as "physics problems" or "biological systems."
*   **Competitive/High-Stamina Hobbies:** There is a striking correlation between high exits and **competitive sports** or high-precision hobbies. Specifically:
    *   **Martial Arts/Boxing:** (Founders 619, 640).
    *   **Competitive Chess/Poker:** (Founder 566).
    *   **Competitive Basketball/Athletics:** (Founder 564).
*   **Direct/Blunt Communication Style:** High-exit bios frequently mention a "no-bullshit filter," being "blunt," or "tactical bluntness" (Founders 566, 575). They prioritize "truth over tact."
*   **Sector Focus:** High exits in this batch are concentrated in **Cybersecurity, Infrastructure/DevTools, and Biotech/Life Sciences.**

### 2. Small or Zero-Exit Founder Features ($0 - $2M)
Founders with failed ventures or nominal exits (e.g., Founder 568, 569, 570, 574, 580) often display "false positive" signals that look good on paper but correlate with poor outcomes:

*   **The "Chief of Staff" or "Strategy Consultant" Trap:** Founders who moved directly from Chief of Staff or Strategy roles (McKinsey, BCG, Bain) without a significant "individual contributor" technical stint often fail (Founders 568, 570, 573, 584). They are "systems thinkers" who lack "ground-level execution."
*   **The "Non-Profit" Detour:** A significant number of $0-exit founders spent time in **educational non-profits or social impact** roles early in their careers (Founders 573, 574, 583, 584, 586, 589). While noble, this often signals a lack of the "ruthless optimization" found in high-exit profiles.
*   **Over-Education/Academic Drifting:** Founders with a **JD (Law Degree)** or an **MD** who are *not* building in a highly regulated space (like InsurTech/HealthTech) tend to have $0 exits (Founders 568, 570, 574, 586).
*   **"Soft" Hobbies:** Low-exit founders are more likely to be described as **"Documentary Enthusiasts," "Creative Writers," "Pottery/Clay workers," or "Bakers"** (Founders 569, 573, 580, 583, 587, 591). These hobbies are reflective/solitary rather than competitive.
*   **The "Optimism" Bias:** Bios that emphasize "relentless optimism," "cheerfulness," or "consensus-building" (Founders 580, 591, 600) are highly correlated with $0 exits. High-exit bios favor "detachment" or "clinical precision."

### 3. Surprising & Non-Obvious Signals
*   **The "Crossword/Puzzle" Signal:** Founders who obsessively solve **crossword puzzles or Sudoku** (Founders 576, 583, 585, 610) have a high failure rate in this batch. This suggests a preference for "closed-system" problems with defined answers, whereas high-exit founders prefer "open-system" competitive games (Poker/Chess).
*   **The "History Buff" Correlation:** Founders who are "History Buffs" or draw parallels to the "Roman Empire/Industrial Revolution" (Founders 563, 565, 587, 611) are almost exclusively in the $0 or low-exit category. They may be prone to over-intellectualizing the market.
*   **The "Home Brewing/Fermentation" Marker:** This is a neutral-to-negative signal. While it shows process-orientation, it appears frequently in $0-exit bios (Founders 579, 589, 594).
