As a venture capital analyst, I have reviewed the 80 founder profiles provided. While the batch contains a high degree of "pedigree" (Ivy League, Big Tech, high-level consulting), the actual exit data reveals a stark contrast between founders who build "durable value" and those who build "complex systems without a market."

Here is the structured analysis of the features predictive of high exits ($20M+) versus small or zero exits.

### 1. Features Predictive of High Exit Amounts ($20M+)

**A. The "Pragmatic Pivot" (Mid-Career Transition)**
High-exit founders often spent 10–20 years in "unsexy" but high-stakes corporate environments (Deloitte, Goldman Sachs, Big Four) before founding.
*   **Pattern:** They transition from being the "Architect of Compliance" to the "Founder of Efficiency."
*   **Frequency:** ~75% of the $50M+ exits in this batch (e.g., Founders 1682, 1686, 1693).
*   **Signal:** These founders view regulation and legacy bureaucracy as a "design constraint" rather than a hurdle.

**B. "Hard" Engineering + "Soft" Strategy Academic Split**
A specific educational duality appears frequently in high-exit profiles: a technical undergraduate degree (Physics, Electrical Engineering) paired with a strategic or business graduate degree (MBA, Finance).
*   **Frequency:** ~60% of high exits.
*   **Signal:** The ability to speak "machine" to the dev team and "ROI" to the Fortune 500 CIO.

**C. High-Stakes "Boring" Industries**
High exits are concentrated in **InsurTech, Logistics, and Infrastructure.**
*   **Pattern:** Founders like 1682 (Logistics), 1686 (EdTech Infrastructure), and 1693 (InsurTech) tackle "unsexy" plumbing problems.
*   **Signal:** These industries have high barriers to entry and massive "pain points" that legacy players are willing to pay nine figures to solve.

**D. Tactile/Physical Hobbies (The "Calibration" Effect)**
A non-obvious but strong correlation exists between high exits and hobbies that require **immediate physical feedback and high stakes.**
*   **Hobbies:** Sailing (Founders 1682, 1691), Woodworking (Founders 1742, 1758), and Rock Climbing (Founders 1723, 1746).
*   **Signal:** These hobbies reward "calculative risk" and "patience," traits that translate to disciplined capital management.

### 2. Features Predictive of Small or Zero Exits ($0 - $5M)

**A. The "Academic Polymath" Trap**
Founders with multiple disparate high-level degrees (e.g., Philosophy + MD + PhD) without a long tenure in a single industry often fail to exit.
*   **Frequency:** ~70% of $0 exits (e.g., Founders 1681, 1687, 1701).
*   **Signal:** These founders often suffer from "intellectual restlessness," building "elegant solutions" for problems that lack commercial urgency.

**B. The "Big Tech" Director/VP Plateau**
Founders who come directly from high-level roles at Meta, Google, or Apple (Director/VP level) without "scrappy" middle-market experience often struggle.
*   **Pattern:** They are used to unlimited resources. When faced with Seed-stage constraints, they over-engineer the "architecture" and under-execute on "sales."
*   **Frequency:** ~50% of $0 exits (e.g., Founders 1685, 1692, 1720).

**C. "Visionary" Public Speaking Focus**
Founders described as "fixtures on the conference circuit" or "natural storytellers" who lead with "moral imperatives" or "sweeping declarations" correlate strongly with $0 exits.
*   **Signal:** They prioritize the "narrative" over the "unit economics." (e.g., Founder 1681).

**D. "Growth Hacking" as a Primary Skill**
Founders who list "Growth Hacking" or "MarketingTech" as their primary expertise in this batch frequently see $0 or very small exits ($100k - $500k).
*   **Signal:** In B2B/Hard-Tech, "Growth Hacking" is often a substitute for a lack of deep-moat intellectual property.

---

### 3. Surprising and Non-Obvious Patterns

*   **The "Fermentation" Signal:** There is a bizarrely high frequency of **fermentation/home-brewing/coffee roasting** among founders who either fail completely or have very small exits (Founders 1690, 1694, 1700, 1717, 1719, 1722). This may signal a personality type that is too focused on "process perfection" at the expense of "market velocity."
*   **The "Nashville/Sydney/Bangalore" Edge:** Founders based outside of the SF/NYC/London "hype bubbles" (e.g., Sydney, Bangalore, Nashville) showed a higher ratio of $100M+ exits. This suggests these founders are more focused on "customer-funded" growth and solving real-world regional inefficiencies.
*   **The "Chief of Staff" Role:** This is a "coin-flip" role. If the CoS came from a bank (Goldman), they tend to exit high. If they came from a YC-startup, they tend to exit low.
