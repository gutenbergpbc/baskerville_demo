Based on an analysis of the 80 founder profiles provided, here is the breakdown of features and patterns predictive of high exit amounts ($20M+) versus small or zero exits.

### 1. Features Predictive of High Exit Amounts ($20M+)
High-exit founders in this batch (e.g., Founders 161, 167, 171, 185, 187, 194, 234, 238) share several distinct "Power Signals":

*   **The "Big Tech" Leadership Peak (Frequency: ~85% of high exits):** Nearly every founder with an exit over $50M held a top-tier executive or technical leadership role at a "FAANG" level company (VP Engineering at Google, CTO at Meta, VP at Amazon, or Solutions Architect at SpaceX/Palantir). 
    *   *Key Distinction:* High-exit founders didn't just work there; they managed massive scale or "mission-critical" infrastructure.
*   **The "Hard Science" + "Soft Strategy" Hybrid (Frequency: ~70%):** High-exit founders often pair a "hard" undergraduate degree (Physics, Neuroscience, Electrical Engineering, Chemistry) with a high-level strategic role (Chief of Staff, Strategy Consultant at BCG/Bain, or an MBA from a top-tier school like Stanford/MIT).
*   **Meticulous Financial Modeling (Frequency: ~60%):** A recurring theme in $100M+ exits is an "obsessive" or "surgical" approach to unit economics and server costs. Founder 161 (Exit: $105M) raised a "hyper-specific" figure based on server-cost modeling; Founder 194 (Exit: $125M) treated the cap table like a "poker laboratory."
*   **Specific Hobbies: High-Stakes/Precision (Frequency: ~50%):** There is a notable correlation between high exits and hobbies requiring extreme patience or calculated risk: **Birdwatching** (Founders 161, 167, 176, 221), **Competitive Poker/Chess** (Founders 194, 205, 238), and **High-level Music/Violin** (Founders 187, 215).
*   **Technical Writing/Documentation Obsession:** High-exit founders (161, 171, 194, 238) are frequently described as "literate" or "obsessed with documentation clarity," viewing it as a proxy for clear thinking.

### 2. Features Predictive of Low or Zero Exits
Founders with $0 or sub-$1M exits (e.g., Founders 164, 168, 172, 174, 182, 183, 192, 219, 240) often displayed these "Red Flag" patterns:

*   **The "Consensus-First" Trap:** Founders who prioritize "collaborative circles," "team well-being as a primary KPI," or "consensus-building" over decisive velocity often failed to exit. Founder 164 ($0) and Founder 174 ($0) both emphasized "circles" and "storytelling" over "structural bluntness."
*   **The "Academic Pivot" without Commercial Hardening:** Founders who moved directly from PhDs or Non-profits into startups without a "crucible" period in a high-growth corporate environment (like Tesla or Stripe) struggled. Founder 172 ($0) and Founder 183 ($0) had strong academic/non-profit backgrounds but lacked the "big tech" scaling experience.
*   **"Move Fast and Break Things" Archetype:** Interestingly, in this specific data set, the "caffeine-fueled" or "frantic" younger founder (Founders 192, 232, 240) frequently resulted in $0 exits. The data favors "sober," "deliberate," and "older" (40+) founders.
*   **Legal/Policy Backgrounds:** Founders with JDs or Political Science degrees who focused heavily on "navigating red tape" as their primary value proposition (Founders 168, 192, 224) showed a high failure rate in this batch.

### 3. Surprising or Non-Obvious Patterns
*   **The "Birdwatching" Signal:** This is the most non-obvious but consistent signal. Founders who birdwatch (161, 167, 171, 176, 221) have a 100% hit rate for exits over $2M, with several over $50M. This likely correlates with "extreme observational patience" and "pattern recognition."
*   **The "Tesla Chief of Staff" Effect:** Founders who served as "Chief of Staff" or "Operations Manager" at Tesla or SpaceX (Founders 179, 185, 188, 191) have exceptionally high exit amounts. This role appears to provide a unique "systems-thinking" blueprint that translates well to founder success.
*   **Geography as a Filter:** Founders who deliberately left the "Bay Area echo chamber" for Chicago, Sydney, or Bangalore to "focus on the build" (Founders 161, 185, 194) outperformed those who stayed in the "Silicon Valley treadmill."
