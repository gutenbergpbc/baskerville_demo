Based on the batch of 80 founder profiles provided, here is an analytical breakdown of the features and patterns predictive of high-exit outcomes ($20M+) versus small or zero exits.

### 1. Features Predictive of High-Exit Outcomes ($20M+)
High-exit founders in this dataset (e.g., Founders 1621, 1656, 1658, 1659, 1666, 1669, 1674) share several distinct "Power Clusters":

*   **The "Elite Generalist" Rotation (Frequency: ~75% of high exits):** High-exit founders rarely stayed in one functional silo. They typically rotated through **Engineering, Strategy (Consulting), and Operations/Product** at "Tier 1" firms (Stripe, Google, Goldman Sachs, McKinsey). 
    *   *Example:* Founder 1658 (PhD Chemistry + Nonprofit + HealthTech VP + Stripe + $236M exit).
*   **Humanities/Social Science Undergrad + Hard Science/Tech Graduate (Frequency: ~60% of high exits):** A recurring "non-obvious" signal is an undergraduate degree in **History, Philosophy, English, or Political Science**, followed by a technical Master’s/PhD or a high-level engineering role. This suggests superior "systems thinking" and communication skills.
    *   *Example:* Founder 1656 (History at Toronto + CS at Caltech + $167M exit); Founder 1674 (English Lit + Morgan Stanley + $23M exit).
*   **"Low-Noise" Hobbies / Strategic Games (Frequency: ~80% of high exits):** High-exit founders gravitate toward hobbies requiring extreme patience, pattern recognition, or high-stakes calculation: **Competitive Bridge, Sailing, High-altitude Trekking, and Chess.**
    *   *Example:* Founder 1621 (Sailing + $87M exit); Founder 1666 (Sailing + Bridge + $64M exit).
*   **Directness/Unvarnished Honesty (Frequency: ~70% of high exits):** Descriptions of these founders often include terms like "blunt," "uncomfortable honesty," "no social filter," or "mathematically offensive." They prioritize the "truth of the data" over social cohesion.

### 2. Features Predictive of Low or Zero Exits
Low-exit or failed founders (e.g., 1601, 1602, 1603, 1604, 1606, 1612, 1618, 1629) often display "The Specialist Trap":

*   **The "Builder-Only" Loop (Frequency: ~65% of low/zero exits):** These founders are often *too* close to the metal. They are described as "submitting pull requests at 2 AM" or "personally rewiring smart locks." While admirable, this often correlates with a failure to scale the CEO role into a strategic/capital-allocator role.
    *   *Example:* Founder 1606 (Materials Science PhD + $52k exit); Founder 1601 (Birdwatching/Engineering + $274k exit).
*   **"Soft" Engineering/Design Backgrounds without "Hard" Finance (Frequency: ~50% of low/zero exits):** Founders coming from **Environmental Science, Design, or Biology** who *did not* supplement that with a stint in high-finance (Goldman/Morgan Stanley) or a "Tier 1" growth engine (Stripe/Uber) tended to have smaller exits.
*   **The "Visionary" Narrative (Frequency: ~40% of low/zero exits):** Founders described as "charismatic," "storytellers," or "evangelists" without a corresponding "cold/surgical" data reputation often failed to exit or had negligible returns.
    *   *Example:* Founder 1633 (Visionary Architect + $22k exit).
*   **Highly Specific/Niche Hobbies (Frequency: ~45% of low/zero exits):** Hobbies like **Birdwatching, Pottery, Baking, and Kayaking** appear frequently in the $0–$1M exit range. While these show focus, they lack the competitive/adversarial element found in Bridge or Poker.

### 3. Surprising or Non-Obvious Patterns
*   **The "Stripe" Effect:** In this specific dataset, a tenure at **Stripe** (especially in Engineering or Product) is the single most consistent predictor of a high-value exit. It appears to be a more potent signal than Google or Meta.
*   **The "Chief of Staff" Role:** Founders who served as a "Chief of Staff" at a decacorn (Amazon, Tesla, Microsoft) have a significantly higher hit rate for $20M+ exits than those who were "Directors of Engineering." This suggests the CoS role provides a better "CEO-readiness" training ground.
*   **Geography vs. Outcome:** Founders who moved *away* from the Bay Area to "secondary" hubs like **Stockholm, Denver, or Nashville** actually show higher exit amounts in this batch, likely due to lower burn rates and higher capital discipline.
