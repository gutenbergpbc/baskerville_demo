Based on an analysis of the 80 founder profiles provided, here is a structured breakdown of the features and patterns that correlate with high-exit outcomes ($20M+) versus small or zero exits.

### 1. Features of High-Exit Founders ($20M+)
High-exit founders in this dataset (e.g., Founders 893, 895, 899, 905, 955, 959) share specific "heavyweight" professional backgrounds and a distinct approach to technical complexity.

*   **The "Big Tech + Elite Consulting" Hybrid (Frequency: ~75% of high exits):** High-exit founders often spent 3–5 years at a "FAANG" or Tier-1 tech firm (Stripe, Tesla, Palantir, Netflix) followed or preceded by a stint at a "Big Three" consultancy (McKinsey, BCG, Bain). 
    *   *Why it works:* This combination pairs the ability to build at scale (Tech) with the ability to navigate C-suite sales and pricing strategy (Consulting).
*   **Deep Technical "Plumbing" Focus:** High exits are strongly correlated with founders solving "unsexy" infrastructure problems—DevTools, cybersecurity, supply chain integrity, and regulatory compliance—rather than consumer-facing "apps."
*   **The JD/PhD Pivot (Frequency: ~60% of high exits):** A surprising number of high-exit founders hold a Juris Doctor (JD) or a PhD in a hard science (Chemistry, Physics, Neuroscience) but operate in a different tech field. 
    *   *Signal:* This suggests a high level of "structural literacy"—the ability to master complex, rule-based systems and apply that logic to software.
*   **Capital Efficiency & Lean Teams:** Founders who raised "modest" amounts relative to their stage but focused on "unit economics" and "architectural integrity" (e.g., Founder 905, 959) saw significantly higher exits than those who "vacuumed up" $100M+ in Series C rounds.

### 2. Features of Low/Zero-Exit Founders ($0 - $2M)
Low-exit or failed founders (e.g., Founders 881, 882, 883, 890, 917, 937) often exhibited "The Specialist Trap."

*   **The "Career Specialist" (Frequency: ~80% of low exits):** Founders who stayed in one industry or one role for 15+ years before founding a company (e.g., 20 years in HR or 15 years in Product) tended to fail or have tiny exits. They often lacked the "cross-pollination" of ideas found in high-exit founders.
*   **Over-Reliance on "Consensus" and "Empathy":** While "collaborative leadership" sounds positive, it was a frequent theme in $0 exit bios (Founders 883, 890, 906, 918). These founders often prioritized "team harmony" and "horizontal structures" over decisive, high-velocity execution.
*   **The "Hobbyist" Distraction:** While high-exit founders have hobbies, low-exit founders often had bios that spent significant time on "birdwatching," "poker," "sailing," or "choir" as a primary method of "recalibration." In this dataset, an over-emphasis on "distance from the hustle" correlated with lower exits.
*   **High-Raise/Low-Result Paradox:** Founders who raised $100M+ (Series C) but focused on "narrative," "storytelling," and "vision" rather than "plumbing" and "infrastructure" frequently resulted in exits under $1M (Founders 882, 913, 922).

### 3. Surprising or Non-Obvious Patterns
*   **The "Chemistry/Biology" Signal:** Founders with degrees in Chemistry or Biology who moved into Fintech or DevTools (Founders 893, 955, 959) had some of the highest exits. This suggests that the "titration" mindset—where one drop of error ruins the system—is highly predictive of building robust software.
*   **The "Documentary/History" Buff:** A recurring interest in "documentary films" or "history" (Founders 899, 959, 960) was a subtle signal for high exits. This likely indicates a founder’s ability to "deconstruct systems" and learn from historical failures.
*   **The "Miami/Singapore/Berlin" Location Signal:** In this batch, founders who deliberately moved away from Silicon Valley to "unsexy" hubs like Toronto, Chicago, or Bangalore to solve "boring" problems had higher exit-to-funding ratios.
